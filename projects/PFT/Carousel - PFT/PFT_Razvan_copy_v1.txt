STEP 1: MARK KEY LEVELS BEFORE THE NEW YORK OPEN

Razvan maps out:
• Previous day highs and lows
• Previous week highs and lows
• Session highs and lows
• Value area highs and lows

His favourite opportunities appear when multiple key levels overlap in the same area.

SLIDE 3
STEP 2: WATCH HOW PRICE REACTS

Reaching a key level isn’t Razvan’s entry signal.
He waits to see what price does when it gets there.

DISPLACEMENT
Price moves aggressively through the level.

CONSOLIDATION
Price starts chopping around the area.

REVERSAL
Price starts rejecting the level.

The reaction determines what Razvan looks for next.

Visual: Three mini charts showing the same key level and three distinct reactions. Label constructed charts as illustrative examples.

SLIDE 4
STEP 3: WAIT FOR REVERSAL CONFIRMATION

Razvan prefers reversals because they offer higher reward-to-risk ratios and clearer targets.

A rejection alone isn’t enough. One setup he demonstrates follows this sequence:

Price reaches a key level
↓
A fair value gap forms through it
↓
The fair value gap is inverted
↓
Price closes back through the level
↓
Razvan enters on confirmation

When trading Nasdaq around the New York open, Razvan says a 5-minute inversion is ideal for him. He can refine his execution on the 1-minute chart.

Visual: One chart with numbered annotations matching the sequence. Confirm the sequence against the episode before drawing it. Label a constructed chart as an illustrative example.

SLIDE 5
STEP 4: LET THE WINNERS RUN

Razvan uses logical levels, such as session highs and lows, as targets.

Once price reaches the target, he switches to the 1-minute chart and trails his stop above each new candle as the move continues.

Razvan says this simple rule can sometimes generate enough P&L to make up for the entire week.

Visual: A short trade reaches the London low and continues lower, with trailing stops above successive candles. Confirm that this is a short-specific example before final production. Label a constructed chart as an illustrative example.
